# Live tuning overlay

The shell is a menu bar, a navigator stacked on an inspector in one side column, an optional help column, and a timeline strip. The menu bar is File, Edit, View, Project, Settings, and Help, and lists commands, checks, and the Settings and Project pages. The navigator holds only the visual composition, and the inspector holds only the settings rows of the selected page. Roots are Overlay, Post-FX, Pattern Mask, Timeline, and Layers. Settings and Project pages (`config_pages.py`) have no navigator row: their menu items open them in the inspector with a `Settings > Preview` style breadcrumb and leave no navigator row selected (`NavigatorControls.open_page`). Reconcile keeps an open config page; preset curation drops Project pages. Each layer has Milkdrop Preset, Preset Switching, Compositing, and Cleave Effects pages; Preset List sits one level under Preset Switching, not expandable, shown while the switching trigger is not Off. The user opens and closes navigator nodes with the twisty, Left/Right, and a click on the label. Selecting a node only opens its ancestors, and Layers starts open. Sub-rows draw tree lines in the indent gutter (`navigator_tree_rails`, `navigator_tree_lines`), and each stem drops from the parent's twisty. A click on an expandable label selects it and toggles it. Overlay, Post-FX, Pattern Mask, Timeline, and layer rows carry clickable on/off and lock icons (`NavNode.toggles`), plus solo on Overlay and layers, and each card row has its own on/off icon. Those pages list the same switches as value rows (`SECTION_SWITCH_KINDS`), and each card page opens with its own on/off row; other child pages have none. A click on an icon selects its row without opening or closing it. Preset curation has no icons and keeps the layer's text badge and lock mark. A child page's E, S, and L keys act on the parent that owns it (`node_control_descriptor`). Settings chooses a side for the navigator column and a side for help. The default puts the navigator column on the left and help on the right. When they share a side, help sits at the window edge and the navigator column stays against the visualiser. The navigator is only as tall as its rows, and the inspector fills the rest of the column and keeps at least half the band, until the user drags the navigator/inspector boundary (`editor.navigator_height`, 0 fits rows). A dragged height leaves each side at least one row. Help is an ImGui column. It does not cover the visualiser. Column sides, stack width, help width, navigator height, and UI scale are editor preferences. UI scale (`editor.ui_scale`, percent) drives every chrome font and spacing through `theme.ui_scale()`; read `tuning_ui_metrics()` / `timeline_ui_metrics()` / `ui_px()` at draw time, never at import. Layer number keys select the matching navigator row, and Ctrl+, opens Settings > Preview. Session state becomes view state; inspector pages come from section defs in `row_sections.py`. Warning and error toasts draw centered on the transport bar, only as wide as the message plus a space on each side. Enter, or a click on the toast, clears a timed toast. A pinned error has no click. When the selection changes and `editor.highlight_new_page` is on, the inspector title runs the same accent swipe (`AttentionClock`, `notification_attention`); the bar spans the panel width, and a title wider than the column scrolls to its end at reading pace, with the bar held long enough to pause before and after (`attention_scroll_sec`, `attention_scroll_hold_sec`, `attention_scroll_offset`). Overlay Latin text uses bundled DejaVu Sans Mono; icons use Material Icons in `assets/fonts/`.

## Where new fields go

A new field is a page child or a command. Pages are `SectionDef`s in `row_sections.py`, mapped to navigator ids in `inspector.py` (`_PAGE_SECTIONS`, `_LAYER_PAGE_SECTIONS`):

- Settings: `SETTINGS_PREVIEW_SECTION`, `SETTINGS_UI_SECTION`
- Project: `PROJECT_MILKDROP_SECTION`, `PROJECT_COMPOSITOR_SECTION`, `PROJECT_RESOLUTION_SECTION`, `PROJECT_RENDER_SECTION`
- Overlay: `OVERLAY_PAGE_SECTION`, `OVERLAY_OPENING_CARD_SECTION`, `OVERLAY_CLOSING_CARD_SECTION`
- Post-FX: `POST_FX_PAGE_SECTION`, `POST_FX_FADE_SECTION`, `POST_FX_HIGHLIGHT_ROLLOFF_SECTION`, `POST_FX_CHROMA_BOOST_SECTION`, `POST_FX_LIMITER_SECTION`
- Pattern Mask: `PATTERN_MASK_PAGE_SECTION`, `PATTERN_MASK_PATTERN_SECTION`, `PATTERN_MASK_EDGES_SECTION`
- Timeline: `TIMELINE_PAGE_SECTION`, `BEAT_BAR_GRID_SECTION`, `TIMELINE_CUTS_SECTION`, `SONG_MARKERS_SECTION`
- Layers: `LAYERS_SECTION`, and per layer `TRACK_SECTION` (preset curation uses `CURATION_LAYER_SECTION`), `TRACK_MILKDROP_PRESET_SECTION`, `TRACK_PRESET_SWITCHING_SECTION`, `TRACK_PRESET_LIST_SECTION`, `TRACK_COMPOSITING_SECTION`, `TRACK_EFFECTS_SECTION`

A new page needs a navigator entry in `navigator.py` (or, for a Settings or Project page, an entry in `config_pages.CONFIG_PAGES`, which adds its menu item), a section mapping in `inspector.py`, and a header mapping in `row_hotkeys.py`.

The inspector has no open or closed state. A block nested in a page through `SectionNode.subsection` draws a static, non-navigable heading with its rows indented under it.

Conditional rows are siblings shown or hidden by a value predicate. The view-state structure includes or omits them at build time and rebuilds only when `view_state_structure_signature()` changes. A field that gates row presence belongs in that signature; leave value-only fields out. Add a `test_structure_signature_invalidates_on_*` test in `tests/cleave/viz/test_view_state_structure.py`.

New rows use `RowKind`, `RowSpec`, and `SectionNode`. Do not add a per-`RowKind` label or mutation branch in `controls.py`. Overlay chrome lives in `overlay_primitives`. `render_visibility_icon` stays in `row_present_renderers.py`.

The timeline strip stays its own host in `timeline_overlay.py`. Selecting a strip row (entering Timeline, or Up/Down there) sets `selected_id` to `timeline.row:<slot>` (`navigator.timeline_row_node_id`), an inspector page with no navigator row like a config page. It edits that row's selected cue (`TIMELINE_ROW_SECTION`, read from `TimelineBlock.selected_cues`). The heading names a start cue (on) or an end cue (off); an end cue shows only time and cut, and no selection shows no cue rows. The cue kind per slot is in the structure signature. The strip's cue keys and these rows share `timeline_cue_edits.py`; add a cue edit there, not in `TimelineControls`. A row with nothing to edit sets `RowSpec.available` and draws disabled. Closing the strip returns it to Timeline; navigator arrows start from Timeline. The focused strip row draws the shell's selected wash and focus outline.

## Parameterized actions

The inspector holds live properties. Commands are buttons. A command that needs parameters opens one dialog, and its label ends in "...". Nothing in the inspector waits for a later Apply.

A button is a command in `commands.py`, placed with `SectionNode(commands=...)` or `SectionDef.trailing_commands`. Commands that have no menu row go in `_INSPECTOR_COMMANDS`. `build_inspector_rows` moves every button row to the bottom of the page, in page order, under a `DIVIDER_ROW` (a thin, non-navigable rule shorter than a row; one also sits under the page title). `help_content._INSPECTOR_COMMAND_KINDS` maps a command to the `RowAffordance.COMMAND` row that holds its help text.

A property with a fixed list of options sets `RowSpec.choice` (`bool_choice` / `options_choice` in `row_specs/common.py`); a form field is a `FormField` with its options. The option count picks the control (`cleave/choice.py`): three or fewer draw as segments, more as a combo whose list opens on click or Enter (Space in a dialog). Lists order low to high, so Right means more, and every step clamps at the ends. On/off rows are false then true. Give a combo row a `jump` when one change must not repeat side effects. Numbers stay `< value >` steppers, padded to the widest value shown. Drawing is shared in `choice_widgets.py`; do not add a per-dialog control.

Dialogs with parameters use `ModalHost.prompt_form` with a `FormSpec`. Fields edit draft values, so Cancel needs no restore. Use `info` to show what the draft would affect, from a dry run. Confirm-only dialogs use `prompt_confirm` with a verb label; `destructive=True` fills the button red and starts on Cancel.

Text and colour properties are `VALUE_TEXT` and `VALUE_COLOUR` rows. They edit in place through `controls.inline_edit`, which owns the keyboard while active. Multi-line text keeps the text modal. Bulk timeline commands record through `TimelineHistory.recording`.

## Enabled gates the frame path

`enabled=False` on an Overlay, Post-FX, or Pattern Mask header must skip that block in the frame path, not only dim the inspector row. Overlay cards gate per card. Post-FX children must check `session.post_fx.enabled`. New post-FX subsections need an `enabled=False` test.

## Typography

Colors live in `cleave/viz/theme.py`. Use existing roles (`LABEL`, `VALUE`, `DISABLED`, `MUTED`, `LOCKED`) plus the accent constants already there. Do not invent a new color without adding a theme constant.

Label+value rows: prefix in `LABEL`, value in the row value color. Exceptions (settings and project headers, transport, notifications, modals) are deliberate; match nearby rows.

## Mouse pointer

Cleave draws its own OS cursors (`pointer_cursor.py`): black with a white outline, sized by `POINTER_BASE_PX` times the UI scale. Draw code calls `request_pointer(PointerShape...)` inside the ImGui frame, from the same hover test that paints the control's highlight, so the pointer and the highlight agree and a modal blocks both. The request resets to the arrow every frame, the last call wins, and `ImGuiHost.end_frame` applies it. Never call `pygame.mouse.set_cursor` elsewhere.

- Arrow: the default, and anything that only selects (navigator and inspector rows, menus, list rows), plus disabled controls.
- Hand: a press acts now. Buttons, icon toggles, twisties, stepper arrows, segments and combos, colour swatches, the play/pause label, a clickable toast, file picker shortcuts, and the strip's play, REC, eye, and row label.
- Hand means the press would act right now, not only that the control is unlocked. A control that refuses for another reason keeps the arrow: `RowSpec.inert` for an inspector row, `NavToggle.inert` for a navigator icon, and `TimelineControls.strip_target_pressable` for the strip (the layer enabled switch while the timeline is on, REC with no armed row or a locked timeline, an eye on a row with no override outside a take).
- Text: an editable text field.
- Resize EW / NS: column splitters and the navigator boundary, a value being dragged sideways, the transport time and bar, and the strip's time scale, a live scrub, or its scrollbar while zoomed.
- Move: a reorder handle, a row being dragged, and a strip pan. Not allowed: a blocked reorder handle.

Gestures that are not ImGui items (splitters, the strip, live drags) are requested in `ShellHost._request_gesture_pointer`, after the shell windows, so they win over widgets.

## Context menus

A right-click opens a context menu for what sits under the pointer; Shift+F10 or the Menu key opens one for the focused item. `ContextMenuState` (`context_menu.py`, no ImGui) holds the open menu, owned as `controls.context_menu`; `ContextMenuSurface` (`menu_rows.py`) draws it as a non-modal top-layer window, sharing `draw_menu_rows` with the menu-bar dropdown. A menu is a provider: a callable returning `ContextMenuItem`s, `ContextSubmenu`s (one level of child items, drawn beside the row by `ContextSubmenuSurface`), and `CONTEXT_SEPARATOR`, re-run every frame so labels, checks, and enabled state stay live. Items call public controller methods, the same paths as their keys, and name that key in `shortcut`. A disabled item stays listed and sets `hint` to say why. To give a region a menu, add a provider module (like `timeline_context_menu.py`), resolve it from the point in `ShellHost._context_menu_at`, and from the focus in `ShellHost._focus_context_menu`. Providers: `timeline_context_menu.py` (strip row; its selected cue's items come from `cue_context_menu.py`, which edits through `timeline_cue_edits.py`), `timeline_time_menu.py` (the clicked song time on the ruler or a row away from a cue: seek, play, drop a song marker; on a marker, selected first, its items replace those: seek, play, a Type submenu, delete; the ruler adds Undo, Redo, and the timeline commands), `navigator_context_menu.py` (navigator row, hit from the row rects `ShellHost` records while drawing), and `preset_context_menu.py` (a preset list entry or the current preset row in the inspector). `ShellHost` records inspector row rects by descriptor too; `_inspector_menu` maps a row kind to its provider, and a right-click focuses the row first. A right-click on a cue selects it first; elsewhere on the row the menu leaves cue items out and leads with the time items. Build an item from a menu-bar `Command` with `commands.command_menu_item`, so the label, shortcut, and enabled state match the menu bar. While a menu is open it takes every key, and a press outside it only closes it.

Timeline visibility overrides start and stop only from the row menu or Shift+Enter (`TimelineControls.toggle_override`); an overridden row's eye draws yellow (`OVERRIDE_BG`). There is no paused preview.

## Keyboard dispatch

Layered in `input_dispatch.py`. Modal keys first, then an open context menu and an active inline edit, each of which takes every key, then Shift+F10 / Menu. Then Tab / Shift+Tab cycles Menu, Navigator, Inspector, and Timeline. Timeline is skipped when the strip is closed. Then the menu bar, command shortcuts, and Ctrl+Up / Ctrl+Down (previous / next top-level navigator node; no effect while the menu bar is focused). Navigator and inspector own their arrows, Enter, Esc, and PageUp/PageDown. An open strip does not take keys. Timeline takes arrows, Enter, Esc, and cue keys only while that region is active. A left click on the open strip takes the Timeline region, then `TimelinePointer` (`timeline_pointer.py`) acts through `TimelineControls`, from `TimelineOverlay.hit_at`: a ruler press seeks, a row press focuses the row and selects a nearby cue without seeking, and a row double-click seeks. A press on a song marker (`StripHit.marker_index`) selects it through `TuningControls.select_song_marker`, which opens Song Markers with its row focused; on the ruler it also seeks to the marker, and on a row a nearby cue wins. Markers draw live through the ruler and rows, and the selected one flashes like a selected cue. A held ruler press or held second row click scrubs once per frame. The corner play/pause and record buttons call `toggle_transport` / `toggle_record` (Space / R), and a double-click on a row label (number and stem) calls `toggle_arm_row` (A). A press on a row's monitor eye calls `toggle_row_visibility` (the layer number key), which acts only on an overridden row, or an armed row while recording. A right-click on a row or the ruler opens a context menu. Mouse motion sets `timeline.strip_hover`, which washes the hovered label or button. The strip maps a visible time window onto its bar: `timeline.strip_view` (`StripView` in `timeline_view.py`, not persisted; the whole song until zoomed), copied to `TimelineViewState.view_start_sec` / `view_end_sec`. Draw and hit code map time through `time_to_x` / `x_to_time` with that window and skip anything outside it; never map against the song duration. The wheel over the strip goes to `TimelinePointer.wheel` instead of ImGui: it zooms about the time under the pointer, and Shift or a sideways wheel pans. While zoomed, a left drag on a row past `PAN_DRAG_PX` or a middle drag on the ruler or a row pans, and the scrollbar under the rows (`scroll_thumb` / `scroll_track` hits) drags the window. `StripView.follow_playhead` runs once per frame: playback pages the window forward, a paused seek recentres it, and a held gesture (`StripView.held`) suspends it. `h` toggles the help column and stays global. Opening or closing help does not move focus. The help column is not a Tab stop, and a click in it does not move focus. There is no panel region and no main-list focus ring.

`row_hotkeys.handle_row_hotkey` is the per-descriptor path for letter, Delete, and Backspace keys, used by the navigator and the inspector.
